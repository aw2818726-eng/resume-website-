Casual riders ride longer, on weekends, in summer

Google Data Analytics capstone. I analyzed 12 months of Cyclistic trip data (Oct 2025 to Sep 2026) in BigQuery to show how annual members and casual riders differ, so marketing can convert casual riders into members.

1. Ask

Business task: find how annual members and casual riders use Cyclistic bikes differently, so marketing can design a strategy to convert casual riders into annual members.

Stakeholders: Lily Moreno (director of marketing), the marketing analytics team, and the executive team, who approve the recommendations.

2. Prepare

The data is the public Divvy trip data. I used the 12 most recent monthly files (202510 to 202609), each with the same 13 columns: ride_id, rideable_type, started_at, ended_at, start and end station names and IDs, start and end coordinates, and member_casual. Cyclistic is a fictional company, so Divvy is the closest real equivalent.

Privacy: the data has no personal information, so findings describe rides, not individual riders, and cannot show who lives in the area or whether a casual rider has bought multiple passes.

3. Process

I loaded all 12 files into BigQuery with an explicit schema, combined them into trips_all_raw (6,171,785 rows) and left that table untouched. All cleaning went into a new table, trips_clean (6,007,147 rides; 164,638 removed, 2.7%).

Cleaning ruleWhy
Keep rides that started between 2025-10-01 and 2026-09-30Removes rides logged in the wrong month's file (71 rows)
Remove duplicate ride_ids35 duplicates

Trim text and lowercase member_casual and rideable_typeConsistent values for grouping
Keep rides with missing station namesMostly e-bikes; dropping them would bias the comparison, so they are excluded only from the station analysis

Timestamps are already Chicago local time, so I did not convert time zones. The SQL for every step is in the analysis queries and the other scripts linked at the bottom.

4. Analyze and share

I compared the two groups on ride count, ride length, day, hour, month, bike type and stations.

Bike type is not the difference. Both groups mostly use e-bikes (74.2% of casual rides, 68.3% of member rides), so it is not a strong basis for a recommendation.

5. Act: top three recommendations

  1. Target leisure riders at the lakefront stations. Run membership promotions at Navy Pier, Millennium Park, Shedd Aquarium and the other stations where casual riders start most of their rides.
  2. Time campaigns for season and weekend. Run them from May to September and on weekends, when casual riding peaks. Consider a summer or weekend-focused membership tier.
  3. Pitch the membership on value for longer, leisure rides. Casual rides are about 49% longer on average, so a cost-per-ride argument fits. Casual riders do not commute, so show that members use bikes for more than commuting.

Limits of the analysis

  • One year of data, so the seasonal pattern is not confirmed across years.
  • The station ranking excludes the roughly 21% of rides with no start station.
  • The data shows ride behavior, not who the riders are, so it cannot say why casual riders choose not to subscribe.
